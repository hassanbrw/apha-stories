## Topic

A servant girl in the Alpha King's castle has spent three years secretly
signing his personal letters in his name — because he never learned to read
past a childhood injury, a shame he has hidden from everyone, including his
own council. What began as a small kindness (she caught him staring blankly
at a letter he couldn't answer, and quietly offered to help before anyone
noticed) became a private arrangement: she reads him what arrives, he tells
her what to say, and she writes it in a hand close enough to his that no one
has ever questioned it. Over three years of these quiet, unwitnessed sessions
— late at night, in a locked study, just the two of them — something has
grown between them that neither will name, because she is a servant and he
is a King who cannot admit a weakness like this to anyone, let alone to
himself. The danger comes from two directions at once: a rival noble on the
council grows suspicious of why the King always insists this one servant
girl be present for his correspondence, and starts digging for the real
reason; and a forged letter — written in her hand, not his, during a crisis
where he wasn't present to dictate it — nearly starts a war with a
neighboring pack when its wording is challenged. When the truth threatens to
come out (the noble finds proof, or the forged letter's origin is
questioned publicly), the Alpha King has a choice: let her take the blame to
protect his secret, or stand in front of his entire council and admit, for
the first time in his life, that he cannot read — and that the girl who has
been quietly protecting that secret for three years is the reason his
kingdom has survived every crisis a letter ever caused.
