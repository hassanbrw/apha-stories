## Topic

A rival witch cursed the Alpha King a year ago: to everyone in his kingdom,
he appears as an ordinary, forgettable woodcutter — a hired hand nobody
looks at twice — and the curse can only be broken by the one person fated
to see him exactly as he truly is. He has spent a year invisible in his own
court, watching his council make decisions without him, a stranger in his
own castle. The only bright spot is a sharp-tongued firewood-seller at the
edge of the village who has been scolding him for a month over late,
badly-stacked deliveries, never once treating him like anyone important —
because to her, he genuinely isn't. What starts as irritation on his side
(a King, reduced to being lectured about firewood by a woman who has no
idea who he is) slowly becomes the only real, honest relationship he has
left, precisely because she has no reason to flatter or fear him. The
danger comes from two directions: the witch who cast the curse is the same
rival noble angling to seize the throne while the "real" Alpha King is
presumed missing or weak, and is growing bolder now that a year has passed
with no sign of the curse breaking; and the firewood-seller herself is being
quietly targeted once the plotting noble realizes a common woman has been
spending unsupervised time with "some woodcutter" who keeps showing up at
the castle gates asking after the King's business, worried he's stumbled
onto something he shouldn't have. When the King finally risks everything to
tell her the truth before the noble can silence her, the curse's real
test arrives: it was never about her believing his claim — it was about
whether she already saw him clearly enough, all along, for it to matter
either way.
