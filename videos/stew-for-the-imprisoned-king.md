## Topic

Alpha King Darius was thrown into his own dungeon a year ago, framed for the
murder of his own father by the one man with the most to gain from it: his
uncle, Lord Regent Aldric, who has ruled in his place ever since, feeding
the court a story of a grief-maddened prince who snapped and turned kill
on the very king he was born to succeed. There was no real trial, only a
formality staged to look like one, and no execution either — Aldric found
more use in a living, disgraced king locked away where the court could
half-forget him than in a martyr the pack might rally around. Darius has
spent a year in the cold and the dark, stripped of his title in every way
that matters, waiting for a mercy or a blade that never comes. What he
does not know is that every single night, without fail, a servant girl
named Wren slips down to the lowest cells with a bowl of stew, sets it
just inside the bars, and leaves without a word. She was a scullery girl
in his father's kitchens once, one of hundreds he never had reason to
notice, and she has never told him why she does it, never asked for
anything, never even let him see her face clearly in the near-dark. It
started as pity and became something she cannot fully explain even to
herself — the closest thing to defiance a nobody in the kitchens can
manage against a regent who has quietly starved out every voice that
might question him. Aldric's grip on the throne is not as secure as he
pretends: whispers are growing among the older packs that the official
story never added up, and a coalition of loyalist lords has spent the
year quietly building a case and gathering the nerve to move against him.
When that loyalist push finally reaches its breaking point and Darius is
freed in a single chaotic night — guards bribed, gates opened, the regent's
own schemes finally catching up to him — the first thing the newly
restored king does, before he reclaims his crown, before he faces the man
who stole a year of his life, is turn to the guards and ask for the name
of the girl who fed him in the dark for three hundred and sixty-five
nights running. Aldric, cornered and out of options, will not go quietly:
if he cannot keep the throne, he intends to make sure the one person who
never abandoned Darius doesn't live to enjoy whatever comes next.
